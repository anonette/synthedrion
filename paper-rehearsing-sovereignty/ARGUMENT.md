# Rehearsing Sovereignty — argument architecture

Working scaffold for the co-authored article. This file holds the conceptual
skeleton so we agree on the spine before polishing prose. The prose lives in
`DRAFT.md`. Register: matches the meta-sovereignty paper (European Journal of
Futures Research). No em-dashes. Target ~8000 words, Futures / STS venue.

Companion to: Reshef Kera, Natan, Turgeman & Ofek (2026), "Europe's AI future
as a meta-sovereign imaginary," *European Journal of Futures Research* 14:1,
doi:10.1186/s40309-025-00261-9. That paper ends by calling sovereignty a
*dramaturgy* and introduces the agent simulations as a proof of concept. This
article turns that closing gesture into the spine: the roundtable and its
prompts are an instrument for studying performed authority, and running it
forward exposes a mechanism the theory could only describe after the fact.

---

## 1. Empirical warrant

**Object.** Synthēdrion, a human-AI roundtable. Three LLM agents argue as
China, the United States, and the European Union, each running a distinct model
(deepseek-chat-v3, gpt-4.1-mini, ministral-14b) and reading from a curated
per-actor policy corpus (CHIPS Act, EU AI Act, New Generation AI Development
Plan, and the collaborators' own source documents). Humans in the room pose the
opening prompt, interrupt and reprompt mid-debate, and summon guest agents
(a peace-builder, a crypto-native analyst, a critical archivist) to break a
settling consensus. Sessions run in three registers: debate, propaganda-lab
(each turn yields a slogan plus a generated poster), and mirror-world (each turn
yields an official line, a buried reality, and a speculation, closing on a
tabloid front page).

**Three levels, kept distinct.**
- *Observation*: the transcripts, poster artifacts, and front pages the system
  records, and the specific human interventions that preceded each shift.
- *Measurement*: turn counts, which agent spoke, which prompt was in force, which
  guest was summoned, whether a position changed after an intervention. These are
  descriptive traces, not scores of "sovereignty."
- *Interpretation*: that what we are watching is performed authority being
  assembled, sustained, and broken. This is the theoretical claim, and it must
  not be smuggled into the measurement.

**What the design cannot establish.** It cannot forecast geopolitical outcomes.
It cannot measure real state capability or intention. Because the models are
trained on the same public discourse the study examines, it cannot treat agent
output as independent evidence about the world. It studies the *legibility,
circulation, and contestation of sovereignty imaginaries*, not ground-truth
geopolitics. This boundary is load-bearing and stated up front.

**The mechanism-sized problem.** Not "is there an AI Cold War." The narrow
phenomenon: *how a performance of sovereignty is sustained, repaired, and broken
under live contestation* — the maintenance work that a retrospective reading of
states' announcements cannot see.

## 3. The reframe

From tool performance to *apparatus*: the object is not "can an LLM do
geopolitics" but "what does staging, interrupting, and re-prompting a performance
of sovereignty make observable." From isolated output to a *recurrent
system-level tendency* visible only across many runs. From transparent
transmission to the *conditions of legibility* under which a sovereignty claim
reads as credible. Agency and authority are located in the arrangement (models,
corpora, prompts, interface, the humans in the room, the audience, and later
circulation), never in a model alone.

---

## 4. The conceptual gap, stated as a bounded relation

> Meta-sovereignty explains that authority in the AI Cold War is performed
> through signals and fictions rather than held through control, but it
> presupposes a retrospective reading, from states' announcements and pledges.
> Costly-signaling theory names the credibility mechanism but treats the signal
> as a discrete act, leaving unspecified how a performance of sovereignty is
> sustained, repaired, and broken under live contestation. The present object, a
> multi-agent roundtable in which humans interrupt, reprompt, and summon rival
> agents, changes the retrospective condition: it lets the performance be run
> forward, perturbed, and observed as it maintains or loses coherence. The
> proposed apparatus names that maintenance-under-contestation and makes it an
> object of study.

## 5. The proposed concept: **synthetic rehearsal**

Introduced only because the gap is real: no existing vocabulary names a forward,
perturbable object for performed sovereignty.

- **Inheritance.** Meta-sovereignty (authority as the performance of signals and
  fictions) and the dramaturgical turn already present in the source paper.
- **Modifier.** Multi-agent LLM staging, human-in-the-loop intervention, and
  prompt design used as experimental controls.
- **Mechanism.** It externalizes the *maintenance work* of performed
  sovereignty, the continuous labor of keeping a costly signal credible and a
  necessary fiction coherent, and makes the fracture points observable at the
  moment the room pushes.
- **Level.** A method (practice/infrastructure of study) yielding output-level
  evidence: transcripts, poster and front-page artifacts, recorded shifts. Not a
  claim about model internals or real state intention.
- **Scope.** Applies to studying the legibility, circulation, and contestation of
  sovereignty imaginaries. Does not apply to forecasting outcomes or measuring
  capability. Bounded by the training-data reflexivity limit (autophagy).
- **Indicators.** *Supporting*: distinct models converging on the same tropes
  (exportability of a governance model, embedding sovereignty in infrastructure,
  ideals colliding with dependencies); predictable fracture when a human summons
  a counter-agent; position shifts traceable to a specific intervention.
  *Falsifying / complicating*: agents producing identical output regardless of
  corpus or prompt (then the rehearsal stages nothing about the discourse); human
  intervention never altering the trajectory (then it is playback, not rehearsal).

Distinguish the concept from its proxy: a recorded "shift" after an intervention
is evidence for maintenance-under-contestation; it is not the process itself, and
a shift can be an artifact of sampling. Repetition across runs is required before
a shift counts.

## 6. Theory-to-evidence ledger

| Theoretical claim | Observable implication | Evidence or test | Rival explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Performed sovereignty requires continuous maintenance | Positions drift or harden across turns even without new facts, tracking rhetorical pressure not information | Code the transcript for position change and align each to the preceding turn/intervention | The drift is model temperature / sampling noise, not maintenance |
| The performance can surface genuinely new strategic options, not just restate corpora | Novel options appear that are absent from the loaded pages | The drone session's EU "public compute auction house" proposal; verify against the corpus | The option was latent in training data and merely retrieved |
| Human contestation is the instrument's active variable | Summoning a counter-agent predictably fractures a consensus | Compare consensus stability in runs with vs without guest summons | Consensus was unstable regardless |
| The materialist objection is itself performed, not outside the theatre | Material claims (rare earths, GPS, cloud) are deployed as credibility moves, timed to rhetorical need | Trace China's rare-earth-bypass claim and US "ecosystem chokepoint" claim to their rhetorical position | Material control is simply true and non-performative |
| The instrument reproduces the imaginaries it studies (autophagy boundary) | The reflexive prompt ("three models trained on the same internet") yields convergence or evasion, not a demonstrable independent difference | Run the reflexive prompt; inspect whether claimed differences are corpus-attributable | The models do hold genuinely distinct positions |
| Recognition, not capability, decides whose performance counts as sovereign | Global South actors enter agent talk as territory/market/mineral unless explicitly written in as actors | Content-analyse actor vs object framing across sessions | The framing reflects real capability distribution |

Rows with no credible test are marked as a future programme, not a finding.

## 7. Section architecture and co-author anchors

1. **Introduction** — empirical problem (AI Cold War read as a race), limits of
   that framing, the gap. *Anchor: Denisa Reshef Kera.* (drafted)
2. **From control to performance: a genealogy** — Derrida → Fearon → Ezrahi →
   meta-sovereignty, with the compute-as-statecraft intensification. *Anchor:
   Eliyahu Keller (Derrida/textuality), Denisa/Merav/Hila (meta-sovereignty).*
3. **Bridge: from description to apparatus** — the changed condition, the gap,
   synthetic rehearsal. *Anchor: Denisa Reshef Kera.* (drafted)
4. **Research questions** — descriptive first (what is staged and how does it
   fracture), interpretive second (what does maintenance-under-contestation
   reveal about meta-sovereignty).
5. **Method: the roundtable as instrument** — agents, corpora, three registers,
   human interventions and guest agents as controls, the reflexivity limit built
   in. *Anchor: Avi Spitz (infrastructure/territory grounding) + the technical
   apparatus.*
6. **What the rehearsal surfaced** — recurrent patterns; the power lens (who is
   actor vs object) and the backstage layer. *Anchor: Oluwakorede Ajibona
   (Global South), James Usher (shadow economy), Lena Slachmuijlder
   (cooperation counter).*
7. **Discussion** — return to each inherited concept: continuity, revision,
   boundary. The autophagy limit as the honest edge.
8. **Conclusion** — contribution (synthetic rehearsal as a method that makes
   performed sovereignty observable and stress-testable) and non-claim (not a
   forecast, not a capability measure).

## 8. Quality audit (to check before submission)

- Every citation performs a named role in the table above.
- The genealogy shows transformation, not prestige by association.
- Both counterpoints (materialist, cooperationist) are answered with evidence or
  a bounded proposal, not dismissed.
- The gap is narrower than "the AI Cold War."
- Synthetic rehearsal adds a mechanism and a unit of analysis; it does not rename
  meta-sovereignty.
- Observation, measurement, interpretation stay separate.
- No agency attributed to a model alone; recognition and adoption located in the
  arrangement.
- The conclusion states both the contribution and the non-claim.
