# China AI Governance Country Profile (Alan Turing Institute, 2026)

> Sources: Leone de Castris, Jiang & Wang, The Alan Turing Institute, "AI Governance around the world: China", 2026-07
> Raw: [AI Governance around the world — China](../../raw/newpapers/aish-international-ai-governance-china.md)
> Updated: 2026-08-13

## Strategy and self-image

- China is one of the largest AI economies and an early mover in AI regulation, aiming for the status of the world's most advanced AI innovation centre by 2030 under the 2017 New Generation AI Development Plan's three-step roadmap (match advanced levels by 2020, major breakthroughs by 2025, global leadership by 2030).
- The strategy balances rapid promotion of AI development with structured oversight: strict rules for high-risk applications (anthropomorphic interactive AI services, recommendation algorithms, deep synthesis in internet services) while the private sector drives development in less critical areas.
- The AIDP's six goals include an open and collaborative innovation system, a high-end intelligent economy, a safe intelligent society, strengthened civil-military integration in AI, ubiquitous intelligent infrastructure, and forward-looking arrangements for next-generation AI science projects.

## The regulatory instrument set

- The governance trajectory is a dense, fast-developing set of application-specific regulations issued as departmental rules and interim measures rather than one omnibus law — an agility advantage, letting regulators issue targeted, technically specific requirements quickly.
- Timeline of binding instruments: Provisions on Algorithmic Recommendations (Mar 2022, transparency and user controls), Deep Synthesis Provisions (Jan 2023, labeling and consent for synthetic media), Interim Measures for Generative AI Services (Aug 2023, security assessments and content aligned with core values), AI-Generated Content Labelling Measures (Mar 2025), and Interim Measures for AI Anthropomorphic Interaction Services (Jul 2026).
- Non-binding layers preceded them: Responsible AI Governance Principles (2019) and New Generation AI Ethical Norms (2021) covering the full lifecycle of development, supply, and use.
- Western readers are cautioned that concepts like legal, ethical, safety, and privacy can carry different semantic connotations in Chinese governance discourse than in Western policy debate.

## Standardisation as statecraft

- The national standards system is centrally coordinated by the Standardisation Administration of China (SAC) and organised hierarchically across territorial levels, covering cross-sectoral and sector-specific AI standards.
- State authorities treat engagement in international AI standardisation explicitly as an opportunity to bolster the international competitiveness of the Chinese economy — standards as an instrument of industrial strategy, not neutral technical housekeeping.
