# A Citizens' Track on AI Governance: Agency, Alignment, Accountability

> Sources: Davies, Field Reid, Oman & Wilson, Iswe Foundation, 2026-07
> Raw: [Citizens' Track on AI Governance whitepaper](../../raw/newpapers/2026-07-citizens-track-ai-whitepaper.md)
> Updated: 2026-08-13

## The democratic deficit

- AI governance has a democratic deficit: governance fora have excluded much of the globe, evidence lags impacts and fails to represent most of the world's population, and the AI-related hopes and fears of affected communities are given little space.
- On current trajectories AI risks being misaligned with public interest, human rights, sustainable development, and safety — while the states and firms negotiating its rules face no organized public voice.
- The proposed remedy is a citizens' track: standing infrastructure that links bottom-up capacity building and deliberation with top-down global fora, embedding spaces for public voice from around the world into the architecture of AI governance.

## Why participation is power, not decoration

- The report's power-aware theory of change claims participation delivers three things: alignment (of models and policies with diverse global public interests), agency (individuals and communities gain critical understanding and decision-making roles), and accountability (social license, community audit, and hard civic power).
- Evidence from climate governance and a casebook of participatory AI processes on every continent shows citizens can be brought into high-stakes, highly technical global governance — building legitimacy, cutting through geopolitical and corporate gridlock, and increasing resilience to capture.
- Participation is framed as a counterweight to exactly the state-and-corporate capture that great-power AI politics normalizes: inclusive processes make governance harder to capture because the affected communities hold a direct hand in shaping guardrails.

## The window of opportunity

- The UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance and the 2027 AI Summit in Geneva are named as the key moments where the exclusionary trajectory can be reversed by wiring participatory processes into official fora.
- The ask to power-holders: build political will to embed public participation structurally, not as consultation theater — connecting a growing network of national, local, and grassroots groups already running participatory AI processes.
- For state actors this is a legitimacy argument with teeth: whichever bloc credibly embeds public voice can claim its AI governance is accountable in a way rivals' declarations are not, and whichever ignores it governs a technology reshaping publics that never consented.
