# Chinese Model Export Curbs and the Coming AI Blockade

> Sources: Forbes (Rashi Shrivastava, The Prompt), 2026-07-07
> Raw: [The Chinese AI Blockade Is Coming](../../raw/newpapers/the-chinese-ai-blockade-is-coming.md)
> Updated: 2026-08-13

## The reversal of the export-control story

- Beijing is in talks to curb overseas access to China's leading AI models, many of them open source, inverting the standard narrative in which only Washington weaponizes export controls (Reuters via Forbes, July 2026).
- Recent Chinese gatekeeping moves include ordering Meta to unwind its $2 billion acquisition of the AI startup Manus and blocking foreign investment in Chinese-linked AI entities.
- The strict rules are paradoxically pushing some Chinese founders to relocate to the United States, so the same controls that hoard capability also leak talent.

## Western dependence on Chinese open models

- American AI firms increasingly run on open-source Chinese models, which can be 60 to 90 percent cheaper than leading models from OpenAI and Anthropic and give developers more control over how the models work.
- OpenRouter reports roughly 30 percent of routed model traffic now goes to Chinese models, and some early-stage US startups have moved all of their traffic to models from companies like DeepSeek.
- Firms risk being locked into the Chinese AI ecosystem: once applications are built on a Chinese model, inertia makes migration progressively harder, so a sudden cutoff would strand them.
- A Chinese model blockade would be a tailwind for US open-source alternatives such as Reflection (founded by ex-DeepMind researchers, $25B valuation, politically connected backers) that market themselves as the American answer to Chinese open weights.

## Surrounding signals of the same season

- OpenAI has discussed granting the US administration a 5 percent equity stake, blurring the line between national champion and state asset, and raising alarm for foreign companies that depend on its models.
- DeepSeek is developing its own chip, and Alibaba and ByteDance run chip initiatives to cut dependence on Nvidia and Huawei, extending the decoupling from models down into silicon.
- The compute build-out is entering an improvised phase: Meta is running data centers inside massive tents in Ohio to halve construction time, described in the industry as the race's "Mad Max phase".
