# AI Index 2026: State of the Race

> Sources: Stanford HAI AI Index 2026 (9th edition), 2026-04
> Raw: `../../raw/articles/ai-index-report-2026.md`
> Updated: 2026-06-16

## Overview

This page is shared factual grounding: a current, numeric snapshot of where the AI race actually stands, so all three actors argue from the same baseline rather than from vibes. The headline is a paradox — the United States leads on money and top-tier models while the capability gap with China has nearly closed and China leads on research volume and patents.

## Capability: A Near-Tie at the Top

As of early 2026 the leading U.S. model leads the best Chinese model by only about 2.7%, down from roughly half a percent when DeepSeek-R1 nearly matched o1 in February 2025. The United States produced 59 notable models in 2025 versus China's 35, and industry produced over 90% of notable frontier models. The gap between top closed and top open-weight models has narrowed to about 3.3%, from 15.2% in 2023. Capability is "jagged": top models win International Mathematical Olympiad gold yet read analog clocks correctly only about half the time, and robots succeed at roughly 12% of household tasks.

## Investment: U.S. Dominance, With an Asterisk

U.S. private AI investment reached about $285.9 billion in 2025, more than 23 times China's $12.4 billion. Global private AI investment hit about $344.7 billion, up over 127% year on year, with 28 funding events above $1 billion. But China's roughly $184 billion in government guidance funds (2000 to 2023) means private-only figures badly understate Beijing's true spend; this is a state-directed program, not a venture market.

## Compute, Research, and Talent

Global AI compute has grown about 3.3 times per year since 2022 to roughly 17.1 million H100-equivalents, with NVIDIA supplying over 60%; the United States hosts 5,427 data centers, more than ten times any rival, and AI data-center power capacity reached about 29.6 gigawatts in 2025. China leads research output with about 17.8% of publications and 20.6% of citations, and holds about 74.2% of global AI patent grants versus the U.S. share of about 12.1%, though the United States leads on higher-impact work. Tellingly, AI-researcher inflow to the United States has dropped about 89% since 2017, eroding a key installed base.

## Adoption, Governance, and Footprint

Generative AI reached roughly 53% population-level adoption within three years, faster than the PC or the internet, and organizational adoption is near 88%. Documented AI incidents rose to 362. On governance the EU leads with the AI Act's first prohibitions in force, while the United States has passed the most AI bills (25 since 2016) yet is pivoting toward deregulation. Energy and emissions are rising sharply, exemplified by a single frontier training run emitting on the order of 70,000 tons of CO2-equivalent.

## Relevance for Actor Knowledge Bases

For a U.S. agent, the data supports confidence on capital and top-tier models but warns that the lead is thin and talent is leaking. For a China agent, the publication, patent, open-model, and state-funding numbers justify a narrative of catching up through volume and direction. For an EU agent, the figures confirm the role of rule-maker without frontier model-maker — power through standards, not capability. All actors can cite this page as common ground before diverging on interpretation.
