# AI Has a Territory: Data Centers and Infrastructural Suzerainty

> Sources: Avi Spitz (Bar-Ilan University), "AI Has a Territory: Data Centers, Sovereignty, and the Geopolitics of AI Infrastructure", 2026-06
> Raw: [AI Has a Territory](../../raw/newpapers/ai-has-a-territory-avi-spitz-final-for-submition.md)
> Updated: 2026-08-13

## The core claim

- AI is not placeless: frontier AI depends on physical, expensive, energy-intensive, often privately owned infrastructure — data centers, accelerators, grids, cooling, optical networks, cloud contracts — so AI sovereignty is a territorial and geopolitical problem, not just a regulatory one.
- Infrastructural suzerainty: a state may formally claim authority over AI while practical control rests with corporations, foreign chip suppliers, cloud providers, energy systems, or security arrangements — formal sovereignty without operational sovereignty.
- Compute sovereignty is layered, not binary (Hawkins, Lehdonvirta and Wu 2025): whether compute exists on a country's territory, who owns and operates it, and whose accelerators power it can each have a different answer.
- The test of AI sovereignty is operational, not declarative: can the state secure electricity, cooling, chips, cloud platforms, software access, security, maintenance, and emergency continuity, or is its national AI strategy a political aspiration resting on someone else's infrastructure?

## Evidence of the new geography

- The IEA projects global data-center electricity consumption could more than double to around 945 TWh by 2030, making AI industrial, environmental, and territorial rather than merely digital.
- Compute is migrating to wherever energy, land, capital, and political permission converge: Stargate UAE in Abu Dhabi (first 200MW phase from 2026, 1GW planned), an AWS Saudi cloud region, and PIF-backed HUMAIN data centers in Riyadh and Dammam.
- The geography is contested locally: in California's Imperial Valley a data-center developer sued for access to roughly 260 million gallons of Colorado River water per year, for a project needing more electricity than the whole county consumed in 2024.
- Proposals for floating data centers at sea and orbital data centers show AI's material demands pushing against terrestrial constraints — land, power, cooling, security, and local opposition.

## Governance consequences

- Infrastructure is invisible until it breaks or is contested (Star): the data center disappears behind the chatbot until electricity, water, land, or chips become scarce, and then it becomes politically visible.
- Data centers are strategic infrastructure in the same class as ports, grids, undersea cables, and pipelines: the actor controlling chips, cloud services, updates, or operational continuity exercises power without formal sovereignty, through dependence alone.
- AI regulation must expand beyond model outputs to compute planning, ownership transparency, energy and water accountability, resilience standards, and crisis-access rules — governing the territory of computation, not only its visible layer.
