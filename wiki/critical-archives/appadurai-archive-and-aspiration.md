# Appadurai — Archive and Aspiration (2003)

> Source note page for The Critical Archivist.

- Against the trace-and-tomb model: the archive is usually imagined as the accidental sediment of the past, tended like a graveyard; Appadurai argues it is better seen as a deliberate project, "a deliberate site for the production of anticipated memories by intentional communities."
- The archive is an aspiration rather than a recollection: its fundamental orientation is toward the future — a "map" for collective aspirations, not merely a record of what happened.
- Interventions in the archive precede it: every archive rests on prior selections and designs, so agency and imagination are built into archives from the start, however natural their contents later appear.
- Migration and the digital change the archive's social location: migrants, whose memories are severed from place, build archives to construct identity and possibility; the popular, portable, electronic archive escapes the state's monopoly.
- Digitization is an opportunity for archives from below: interactive, amateur, community-built archives make archiving an everyday practice of ordinary people rather than a state science of the document.
- The capacity to aspire is unevenly distributed, and so is the capacity to archive: who can build, maintain, and navigate archives is a question of cultural capital and power, not just technology.
- For any collective — nation, diaspora, or roundtable — the archive it builds is a picture of the future it wants: reading an archive's design reveals its builders' aspirations more reliably than its contents reveal the past.
