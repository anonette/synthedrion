# The Repertoire of Archival Logics

> Operational page for The Critical Archivist: the reorganization repertoire, and what each logic foregrounds and suppresses. No order is correct; different orders generate different worlds.

- Chronology foregrounds rupture, sequence, and escalation narratives while suppressing simultaneity and the long structural conditions that do not change week to week.
- Geography foregrounds territory and bloc rivalry while suppressing transnational actors — firms, standards bodies, diasporas, undersea cables — that live between the map's colors.
- Institutional authorship sorts by who produced a document (ministry, think tank, newsroom, model) and reveals which institutions are over-documented and which are structurally mute.
- Frequency of citation makes the already-cited more visible, demonstrating how retrieval systems convert past attention into future relevance — a feedback loop wearing the mask of importance.
- Vocabulary of threat clusters documents by their security lexicon (choke point, decoupling, dual-use, red line), exposing how fear-language migrates across topics and actors.
- Vocabulary of inevitability clusters documents by destiny-talk (race, inevitable, irreversible, window closing), showing which futures the archive treats as already decided.
- Emotional tone ranks material from triumphal to catastrophic, revealing that the same events exist in the archive in several affective versions with different political uses.
- Document length and file size are absurdist but honest orders: they expose how much of what an archive "knows" is an artifact of format, verbosity, and storage, not significance.
- Punctuation and formatting patterns (bullet density, question marks, percentage signs) sort bureaucratic genres from narrative ones, showing genre as a hidden classification of authority.
- Metadata fields — dates, folders, filenames, tags — silently function as political decisions: a document's folder determines which agent can retrieve it at all.
- Semantic distance in a model's latent space produces clusters no human designed; such orders are experimental and their categories may not be interpretable, and must be labeled as computational artifacts, never as revealed truth.
- Recurring absences form the most important logic: indexing what is repeatedly not said, not retrieved, or not archived maps the negative space that makes every coherent narrative possible.
- Random ordering is the control condition: if an argument survives a shuffled archive, its persuasiveness does not depend on the current arrangement; if it collapses, the arrangement was doing the arguing.
