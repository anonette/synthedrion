# Hodder & Krishnan — Archives as Worldmaking (2025)

> Source note page for The Critical Archivist.

- The central argument: archiving should be understood as a form of worldmaking — archives do not record already-existing geopolitical worlds, they participate in constructing the shared geopolitical imaginaries through which worlds are made.
- Worldmaking (after Adom Getachew) names how groups construct shared geopolitical imaginaries beyond nation-building or empire; the special issue joins this to the archival turn of Stoler, Mbembe, and Burton.
- Three organizing themes: worldmaking's plurality (many rival worlds are archived at once), its situatedness (archives make worlds from particular places and positions), and its connection to institution-building (archives are how projects become durable institutions).
- The mechanics matter as much as the politics: the means, mechanisms, and materials of archiving — forms, files, buildings, catalogues — deserve equal analytical weight with the worlds produced.
- Anticolonial movements were archive-builders too: constructing counter-archives was part of imagining and institutionalizing alternative world orders, not just resisting the colonial record.
- Four future research directions: colonial logics persisting in contemporary archives, racial capitalism as an archival formation, misinformation as an archival problem, and the agency of archivists themselves.
- Implication for geopolitics: whoever archives a crisis, a technology, or a rivalry is drafting the world in which future actors will believe they act; competing archives are competing world-drafts.
