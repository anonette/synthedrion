# Derrida — Archive Fever: A Freudian Impression (1996)

> Source note page for The Critical Archivist.

- The word "archive" descends from the Greek arkheion, the house of the archons, the magistrates who both guarded official documents and held the power to interpret them; the archive fuses commencement (origin) and commandment (authority) in a single institution.
- Archivization is not passive recording: "the technical structure of the archiving archive also determines the structure of the archivable content even in its very coming into existence." The medium and apparatus of storage shape what can be archived at all.
- "There is no political power without control of the archive, if not of memory. Effective democratization can always be measured by this essential criterion: the participation in and the access to the archive, its constitution, and its interpretation."
- The archive is not a memory but a substitute for memory: it takes place at the site of memory's structural breakdown, an external prosthesis compensating for finite, failing recall.
- Archive fever (mal d'archive) is the compulsive, repetitive desire to return to the origin, paired with the death drive that works silently inside every archive to destroy it; conservation and destruction are entangled in the same gesture.
- Every act of consignation — gathering signs into a single corpus, a single system — imposes an ideal of unity that suppresses heterogeneity; classification is already interpretation.
- The archive is oriented toward the future, not the past: "the archive is a pledge, and like every pledge, a token of the future"; the question of the archive is the question of what is coming, of what a society promises itself to remember.
- If email and new communication technologies had existed, psychoanalysis itself would have been different: the tools of archiving transform the events they record, so infrastructures of memory produce, not merely preserve, the phenomena they document.
