# Anderson — The Case of the Archive (2013)

> Source note page for The Critical Archivist.

- Anderson examines the hospital case file as an archival technology: the unitary dossier that follows a patient made the individual knowable, comparable, and governable — an archive of persons rather than of states.
- Following Foucault: "the examination, surrounded by all its documentary techniques, makes each individual a 'case': a case which at one and the same time constitutes an object for a branch of knowledge and a hold for a branch of power."
- The case file entered the clinic around the same logic as prisons, asylums, barracks, and schools: closed institutions produce files, and files produce the individuals they describe — described, judged, measured, compared, trained, corrected, classified, normalized, excluded.
- The format of the record shapes the knowledge: standardized forms, admission sheets, and progress notes determined what could count as a symptom, an outcome, or a life worth noting.
- Case-making is a genre with selection effects: institutions cluster around "good cases," and what makes a case good — legibility, typicality, teaching value — is an archival judgment that filters which lives become knowledge.
- The dossier travels and outlives its subject: aggregated case files become research archives, statistics, and policy evidence, laundering the power relations of their creation into neutral-seeming data.
- The lesson for any knowledge system: whenever heterogeneous experience is converted into filed, comparable units, an archive is exercising power — the unit of filing (the case, the incident, the data point) is a political artifact.
