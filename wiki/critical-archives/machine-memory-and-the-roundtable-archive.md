# Machine Memory and the Roundtable as Archive

> Operational page for The Critical Archivist: applying critical archival studies to AI systems and to this simulation's own knowledge bases.

- Training corpora, knowledge bases, and retrieval indexes are archives in the full technical sense: products of selection, exclusion, classification, ranking, and access control that precede and condition every generated statement.
- A language model's context window is an archive with a violent appraisal policy: everything outside it is, for the duration of the utterance, not merely forgotten but unsayable.
- Retrieval-augmented agents inherit appraisal power from their pipelines: chunking, embedding, and relevance scoring decide which passages can ground a claim — ranking functions are finding aids, and finding aids are politics.
- In this simulation, each state actor speaks only from its own wiki folder plus shared pages: the folder structure is the treaty; the allowed-path list is a border regime for knowledge; a page unlinked from a hub does not exist for that actor.
- The source notes fed to each actor are capped and keyword-scored against the session prompt, so the prompt itself acts as an appraisal officer, promoting some notes and silently discarding the rest.
- What each actor archives is its aspiration (after Appadurai): China's, America's, and Europe's knowledge bases are portraits of the futures each wants to have been inevitable.
- The roundtable transcript is itself an archive under construction: turns, interventions, shocks, summaries, and discarded outputs are being selected and formatted for replay — the replay is the monument, and the monument is edited.
- Dataset politics are geopolitics: whoever curates the corpora that national models train on is drafting what those models — and the institutions leaning on them — can imagine about rivals, allies, and themselves.
- Machine-generated clusters and rankings often appear as neutral relevance; the critical move is to ask of every retrieval, as of every archive: who selected, what was excluded, and would the argument survive a different order?
