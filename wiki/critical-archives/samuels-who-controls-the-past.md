# Samuels — Who Controls the Past (1986)

> Source note page for The Critical Archivist.

- Opens from Orwell's 1984: "Who controls the past controls the future: who controls the present controls the past" — and argues archivists, who select the surviving record, are among those controllers whether they admit it or not.
- The core proposal is the documentation strategy: instead of passively appraising whatever records happen to arrive, archivists should first analyze what in society ought to be documented — an issue, function, or activity — and then plan across institutions to capture it.
- Modern information is interinstitutional: no single repository's holdings can document phenomena (science, protest, industry) that span government, universities, corporations, and individuals, so collection decisions made in isolation guarantee systemic gaps.
- Appraisal choices are choices about the future's knowledge: each decision about retention silently answers the question of what future researchers will be able to ask.
- The abundance problem is as political as the scarcity problem: in an age of overwhelming record volume, selection becomes more aggressive, and its criteria — rarely examined — do the real historiographical work.
- Documentation strategies require making selection criteria explicit, cooperative, and revisable: the strategy is a mechanism to expose and debate the archive's construction rather than leaving it to accident and precedent.
- Samuels shifts the profession's self-image from custodian to active shaper: the archivist chooses the past that will exist, so the choice must be deliberate, documented, and accountable.
