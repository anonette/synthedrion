# AI Infrastructure as Power

## Overview

This page supplies a conceptual lens, not new facts. Infrastructure-studies theory reframes AI compute, data, and standards as contested, value-laden systems that redistribute power, rather than neutral plumbing. It gives agents sharper language for why control over chips, benchmarks, and protocols is structural power, and why investment alone does not buy leadership.

## Invisible Until Breakdown

Infrastructure is relational and largely invisible in normal use; it becomes visible only when it breaks or is denied. Export controls, chip shortages, and mineral cutoffs are precisely the breakdown moments that suddenly make the installed base visible and political. What looked like background plumbing is revealed as a chokepoint the instant access is withdrawn.

## The Installed Base

Infrastructure is grown, not built: it wrestles with the inertia of an installed base of artifacts, habits, norms, and roles. NVIDIA's CUDA lock-in, TSMC fabrication, existing data-center siting, and grid capacity are the inherited base every actor must build on or around. This explains why a capital lead or a patent lead does not translate instantly into frontier capability — the base resists, and backward compatibility constrains the new.

## The Politics of Standards and Gateways

Standards embody values, simplifications, and treaties; whoever sets them sets the terrain. Benchmarks, model cards, safety evaluations, and interoperability protocols are governance instruments, not neutral measurements. Infrastructure also forms through gateways that let heterogeneous systems interoperate, rather than through one system winning outright — open weights, APIs, model routers, and standards bodies are gateways, and control over them is structural power.

## Redistribution, Contention, and the Lag

Infrastructure shifts the life chances of groups, expanding some and constraining those on the receiving end, and it provokes "repertoires of contention" from outright resistance to a cool politics of studied non-adoption — useful framing for indigenization drives, open-model releases as counter-power, and decoupling. Social and regulatory debate also tends to lag irreversible infrastructural decisions by twenty to forty years, which makes governance structurally reactive. Finally, "master narratives" manufacture unitary global actors — the caution that "the United States stands for democracy" is a construction applies directly to a simulation: treat each bloc as an aggregation, not a single will.

## Relevance for Actor Knowledge Bases

Every actor can use this lens to argue that its rivals' visible advantages rest on fragile installed bases, that standard-setting is a quieter but deeper form of power than model benchmarks, and that the costs of infrastructure fall on invisible labor and peripheral regions. It is a rhetorical toolkit for reframing the material pages on minerals, compute, and energy as questions of power rather than engineering.
