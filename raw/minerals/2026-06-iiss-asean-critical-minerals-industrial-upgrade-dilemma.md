# ASEAN's Critical Minerals and Industrial-Upgrade Dilemma

> Source: IISS Online Analysis, 2026-06. Author: Wakana Asano (Research Associate, API Takano Fellowship).
> URL: https://www.iiss.org/online-analysis/online-analysis/2026/06/aseans-critical-minerals-and-industrial-upgrade-dilemma/
> Note: The full IISS body is member-gated. This file captures the article's structure, its cited claims (attributed to the underlying sources), and the complete list of documents it references.

## Core Argument

ASEAN faces an "industrial-upgrade dilemma": moving up the critical-minerals value chain through downstreaming and refining — led by Indonesia's nickel processing build-out — raises domestic value capture but deepens reliance on Chinese capital and technology, even as it exposes the region to intensifying US–China geo-economic competition. The sharpest expression is that Indonesia's downstreaming "success" was largely financed and operated by Chinese firms, so value-addition and dependreduction pulled in opposite directions.

## Cited Claims (attributed to the article's referenced sources)

- Indonesia accounts for over 60% of global nickel output after its raw-ore export ban drove a processing build-out (Goldman Sachs).
- Chinese firms control roughly 75% of Indonesian nickel capacity (Reuters, 2025-02-05) — downstreaming raised value capture but entrenched Chinese ownership of the midstream.
- The Philippines is the region's largest nickel producer/reserve holder and a key upstream supplier (US Dept of Commerce / trade.gov).
- ASEAN is pursuing a collective "cooperate, not compete" critical-mineral strategy (ERIA), formalized in the ASEAN Minerals Cooperation Action Plan (AMCAP) IV, 2026–2030 (ASEAN Secretariat).
- Critical-mineral supply for the clean-energy transition is highly concentrated (~90% of some processing stages), and new export controls turn concentration into realized supply risk (IEA).
- ASEAN supply is itself concentrated and exposed to chokepoint vulnerabilities (ERIA; Hoover Institution).
- External partners are moving to de-risk: ADB's new approach and new ASEAN critical-minerals financing tools (ADB; SCMP); Japan's Asia Zero Emission Community (AZEC) expansion and a World Bank–Japan critical-minerals cooperation launched 2026-06-01.
- The United States is competing for access through "Pax Silica" critical-minerals diplomacy and the 2026 Critical Minerals Ministerial, including a US–Indonesia trade deal (2026-02) and US MoUs with Malaysia and Thailand (2025-10) (US State Department; White House; IISS).
- Despite diversification efforts, ASEAN's dependence on Beijing for processing capacity and technology persists (GIS Reports).
