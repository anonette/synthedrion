The Cetus incident involved a significant crypto hack targeting a blockchain protocol, resulting in the theft of approximately $223 million USD. The attack was executed on 22 May 2025, exploiting vulnerabilities within the protocol's infrastructure. The immediate financial impact was severe, affecting numerous users and stakeholders associated with the protocol.

The attack exploited a vulnerability in the protocol's smart contract, potentially involving reentrancy or access control failures. The exact exploit mechanism remains under investigation, but initial analysis suggests the use of sophisticated scripts and tools to manipulate contract functions.

Stolen funds were rapidly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple blockchain networks and laundering stages. The attacker utilised various bridges and mixers to obscure the fund trail, eventually reaching centralised exchanges for cash-out. Key infrastructure included the use of high-volume intermediaries and known laundering services.

The attack is suspected to be orchestrated by the APT38 group, known for targeting financial institutions. This attribution is based on the use of similar tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) observed in previous incidents linked to the group.
