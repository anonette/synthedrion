The ChainSwap incident involved a sophisticated crypto hack targeting the ChainSwap protocol on 11 July 2021. The attack resulted in the theft of approximately $4.4 million USD, affecting numerous users and stakeholders. The exploit mechanism involved a vulnerability within the protocol's smart contract infrastructure, allowing the attacker to siphon funds undetected initially.

The attack exploited a smart contract vulnerability, potentially involving reentrancy or access control failures. The attacker utilised a series of transactions to manipulate the protocol's state, enabling the unauthorised transfer of assets. The exact technical details of the exploit remain under investigation, but the rapid execution suggests premeditated planning and a deep understanding of the protocol's architecture.

Stolen funds were initially moved through a series of rapid transactions across multiple wallets, employing techniques such as bridge hopping and mixer usage to obfuscate the trail. The funds were subsequently distributed across various chains and exchanges, including Ethereum and potentially others, to further complicate tracing efforts.

The Lazarus Group, a well-known cybercrime syndicate, is suspected of orchestrating the attack. This attribution is based on the use of similar tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) observed in previous incidents linked to the group. The confidence level in this attribution is medium, pending further corroborative evidence.
