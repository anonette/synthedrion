On 15 July 2025, ArcadiaFi, a decentralised finance (DeFi) protocol, experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $3.6 million USD. The attack targeted the protocol's smart contracts, exploiting a vulnerability that allowed the attacker to siphon funds from the platform. The immediate financial impact was severe, affecting both the protocol's liquidity and its users' deposits.

The attack was executed by exploiting a vulnerability in ArcadiaFi's smart contract code. The attacker utilised a series of rapid transactions to manipulate the protocol's state, potentially involving a reentrancy attack or a flash loan exploit. The precise mechanism involved remains under investigation, but it is clear that the attacker was able to bypass existing security measures to execute the theft.

Following the exploit, the stolen funds were quickly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple wallets. The attacker employed a sophisticated laundering strategy, utilising various blockchain bridges and mixers to obfuscate the fund flow. Key infrastructure used included high-volume intermediaries and known laundering services, indicating a high level of premeditation and technical expertise.

The attack is suspected to have been carried out by the hacker group APT38, known for its advanced persistent threat capabilities and previous involvement in similar incidents. The use of specific laundering techniques and infrastructure overlaps with past APT38 operations supports this attribution, although further evidence is required to confirm their involvement conclusively.
