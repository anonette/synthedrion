On 19 May 2021, the PancakeBunny protocol, a decentralised finance (DeFi) platform operating on the Binance Smart Chain, suffered a significant exploit resulting in the theft of approximately $45 million USD. The attack involved a sophisticated manipulation of the protocol's smart contracts, leading to a substantial financial impact on the platform and its users.

The attack was executed through a flash loan exploit, a common vulnerability in DeFi protocols where attackers manipulate the price of assets within a single transaction block. This exploit involved the use of flash loans to artificially inflate the price of the BUNNY token, allowing the attacker to withdraw excessive amounts of funds from the protocol.

Post-exploit, the stolen funds were rapidly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple wallets and blockchain networks. The attacker utilised various laundering techniques, including bridge hopping and mixer services, to obfuscate the fund flow. Key infrastructure used included the Ethereum and Binance Smart Chain networks, with funds eventually reaching centralised exchanges for cash-out.

While the specific threat actor remains unidentified, the attack exhibits characteristics consistent with known DeFi exploit groups. The use of advanced laundering techniques and infrastructure overlaps with previous incidents suggest a high level of sophistication and experience.
