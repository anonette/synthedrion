On 25 March 2025, a significant crypto hack incident, referred to as "Abracadabra," was executed, resulting in the theft of approximately $12,913,691.00. The attack targeted a blockchain protocol, exploiting vulnerabilities to siphon funds into various wallets. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting both the protocol's operations and its user base.

The attack was executed through a series of rapid transactions exploiting a vulnerability in the protocol's smart contract infrastructure. The specific weakness exploited remains under investigation, but initial analysis suggests potential access control failures or reentrancy issues. The attack involved multiple transactions executed in quick succession, indicating a premeditated strategy.

Stolen funds were initially moved through a series of intermediary wallets, employing techniques such as bridge hopping and mixer usage to obfuscate the trail. The funds traversed multiple blockchain networks and utilised decentralised exchanges (DEXs) for asset swapping. Ultimately, portions of the funds were directed towards centralised exchanges (CEXs) for cash-out attempts.

The threat actor is suspected to be APT38, a group known for sophisticated cyber operations and financial crimes. The attribution is supported by the use of similar tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) observed in previous incidents linked to this group.
