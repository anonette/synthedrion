# Crema Finance — DPRK-attributed incident

> Vector: unknown
> References: Crema Finance – Blockchain Forensics Intelligence Report (https://sandbox.hacksleuths.com/LLM_REPORTS/Crema_Finance/Crema_Finance_report.html)

On 2 July 2022, Crema Finance, a decentralised finance (DeFi) protocol, experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $8.8 million USD. The attack targeted the protocol's smart contracts, exploiting vulnerabilities to siphon funds. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting both the protocol's liquidity and its user base.

The attack was executed through a sophisticated exploit of Crema Finance's smart contracts. The attacker utilised a vulnerability in the contract's logic, potentially involving reentrancy or flash loan attacks, to manipulate the protocol's operations and extract funds. The exact mechanism remains under investigation, but the use of automated scripts and tools is suspected.

Post-exploit, the stolen funds were rapidly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple wallets and blockchain networks. The attacker employed a combination of mixers, bridges, and exchanges to obfuscate the fund trail. Notably, Tornado Cash and other privacy-enhancing tools were used to launder the funds across different chains.

The attack is attributed to the hacker group APT38, known for its sophisticated cyber operations and links to state-sponsored activities. The group's tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) align with those observed in this incident, including the use of advanced laundering techniques and infrastructure overlaps with previous attacks.
