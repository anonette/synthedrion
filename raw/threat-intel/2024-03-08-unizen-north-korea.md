On 8 March 2024, the Unizen protocol experienced a significant crypto hack resulting in the theft of approximately $21 million USD. The attack targeted the protocol's smart contract infrastructure, exploiting vulnerabilities to siphon funds into attacker-controlled wallets. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting both the protocol's liquidity and its user base.

The attack was executed through a series of transactions exploiting a vulnerability in the Unizen protocol's smart contract. The specific exploit mechanism involved manipulating contract functions to redirect funds. The attack likely involved sophisticated scripting and possibly the use of flash loans to amplify the exploit's impact.

Stolen funds were rapidly moved through a series of transactions across multiple wallets and blockchain networks. The attacker utilised various laundering techniques, including bridge hopping and mixer services, to obfuscate the fund trail. Key infrastructure used included the Poly Network and several high-volume intermediary addresses.

While the specific threat actor remains unidentified, the attack's sophistication suggests involvement by a well-resourced group with prior experience in blockchain exploits. The use of advanced laundering techniques and infrastructure overlaps with known threat actors increase the confidence in this assessment.
