On 18 July 2025, CoinDCX, a prominent cryptocurrency exchange, experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $44.3 million USD. The attack was executed by exploiting vulnerabilities within the exchange's infrastructure, leading to unauthorised access and fund diversion. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting both the exchange's liquidity and its user base.

The attack leveraged a combination of smart contract vulnerabilities and infrastructure weaknesses. The exploit involved manipulating transaction sequences to bypass security protocols, potentially involving reentrancy attacks or private key compromises. The exact technical details remain under investigation, but the rapid execution suggests premeditated planning and sophisticated technical capabilities.

Stolen funds were initially moved through a series of rapid transactions across multiple wallets, employing techniques such as bridge hopping and mixer usage to obfuscate the trail. The funds traversed various blockchain networks and utilised decentralised exchanges (DEXs) and mixers like Tornado Cash to further complicate tracing efforts.

The attack is attributed with high confidence to the Lazarus Group, a well-known North Korean cybercrime syndicate. This attribution is based on the use of similar tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) observed in previous incidents linked to the group, including infrastructure overlaps and transaction patterns.
