On 5 March 2025, a significant crypto hack targeted the 1Inch protocol, resulting in the theft of approximately $5,000,000.00 USD. The attack was executed by the hacker group APT38, known for its sophisticated cyber operations. The immediate financial impact was severe, affecting the protocol's liquidity and user trust.

The attack exploited a vulnerability within the 1Inch protocol's smart contract infrastructure. The specific weakness involved was not detailed in the source material, but the rapid execution of transactions suggests a potential flash loan attack or reentrancy exploit. The attack was characterised by high transaction velocity and the use of multiple intermediary wallets to obfuscate fund flow.

Stolen funds were initially moved through a series of rapid transactions across multiple wallets, employing techniques such as bridge hopping and mixer usage to launder the assets. The funds were eventually routed through known laundering infrastructure, including Tornado Cash, before reaching centralised exchanges for cash-out.

APT38 is suspected to be behind the attack, with a high confidence level due to the use of known TTPs and infrastructure overlaps with previous incidents attributed to this group. The group's history of targeting financial institutions and their sophisticated laundering techniques further support this attribution.
