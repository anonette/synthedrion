On 18 December 2020, a significant crypto hack incident, referred to as "Hacken," was executed, resulting in the theft of approximately $7,800,000.00. The attack targeted a blockchain protocol, exploiting vulnerabilities to siphon funds into attacker-controlled wallets. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting both the protocol's operations and its user base.

The attack was executed through a series of transactions that exploited a vulnerability in the protocol's smart contract infrastructure. The specific weakness exploited remains under investigation, but the pattern suggests potential use of reentrancy or access control failures. The attack involved rapid transaction sequences, indicating premeditated execution.

Stolen funds were initially moved from the exploit wallet to intermediary wallets, employing a strategy of rapid multi-hop transactions across various blockchain networks. The funds were subsequently laundered through known mixers and bridges, including potential use of the Poly Network and other high-volume intermediaries.

The Lazarus Group, a well-known cybercrime syndicate, is suspected of orchestrating the attack. This attribution is based on the group's historical tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs), which align with the observed transaction patterns and infrastructure usage.
