The DeltaPrime incident involved a sophisticated crypto hack targeting a blockchain protocol on 15 September 2024. The attack resulted in the theft of approximately $5,980,000.00. The exploit mechanism remains under investigation, but initial assessments suggest a coordinated attack by the APT38 group.

The attack was executed through a series of transactions that exploited vulnerabilities in the protocol's smart contracts. The exact technical details of the vulnerability remain unclear, but the attack involved rapid fund transfers and potential use of compromised private keys.

Stolen funds were quickly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple wallets and exchanges. The attackers utilised known laundering techniques such as bridge hopping and mixer usage to obscure the fund trail. Key exchanges and bridges involved include DeltaPrime - II and various intermediary wallets.

The APT38 group is suspected of orchestrating the attack, with a high confidence level due to overlapping tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) with previous incidents attributed to this group. The use of sophisticated laundering methods further supports this attribution.
