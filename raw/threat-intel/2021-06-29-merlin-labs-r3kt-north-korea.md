On 29 June 2021, Merlin Labs, a decentralised finance (DeFi) protocol, experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $330,000.00 USD. The attack targeted the protocol's smart contracts, exploiting vulnerabilities to siphon funds into attacker-controlled wallets.

The attack was executed through a series of rapid transactions exploiting a vulnerability in the protocol's smart contract logic. The specific weakness exploited involved improper access control, allowing the attacker to manipulate contract functions to redirect funds. The attack did not involve flash loans or price manipulation but relied on direct contract exploitation.

Stolen funds were initially moved from the exploit wallet to intermediary wallets, employing a series of transactions designed to obfuscate the trail. The attacker utilised known laundering techniques such as mixer services and cross-chain bridges to further obscure the fund flow. The funds were eventually directed towards centralised exchanges for cash-out.

The identity of the threat actor remains unknown; however, the sophistication of the laundering techniques suggests involvement of a well-organised group with prior experience in DeFi exploits. The use of multiple offshore entities and complex transaction patterns indicates a high level of operational security.
