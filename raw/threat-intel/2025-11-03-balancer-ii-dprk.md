On 3 November 2025, the Balancer protocol experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $128 million in digital assets. The attack targeted the protocol's liquidity pools, exploiting vulnerabilities in the smart contract infrastructure. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting both the protocol's operations and its user base.

The attack was executed by exploiting a vulnerability in the Balancer protocol's smart contracts, potentially involving reentrancy attacks or price manipulation techniques. The attacker likely used flash loans to manipulate asset prices within the pools, allowing them to drain funds without holding significant capital upfront.

Stolen funds were rapidly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple wallets and blockchain networks. The attacker utilised various laundering techniques, including bridge hopping and mixer usage, to obfuscate the fund flow. Notably, the funds were transferred across several chains and through known mixing services to hinder traceability.

The Lazarus Group is suspected of being behind this attack, given the similarities in tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) observed in previous incidents attributed to them. The use of sophisticated laundering methods and infrastructure overlaps with known Lazarus operations support this attribution.
