# Kucoin — DPRK-attributed incident

> Type: threat-intel incident (SOURCED CLAIM — treat attribution as a claim at the stated confidence, not fact)
> Incident ID: CHA-Kucoin-97
> Timestamp: 2020-09-29T00:00:00Z
> Attribution: DPRK / Lazarus Group (confidence: 0.07)
> Target: Kucoin
> Amount (USD): $45,000,000
> Asset: Ethereum
> Vector: unknown
> References: Kucoin – Blockchain Forensics Intelligence Report (https://sandbox.hacksleuths.com/LLM_REPORTS/Kucoin/Kucoin_report.html)

On 29 September 2020, Kucoin, a prominent cryptocurrency exchange, experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $45 million in digital assets. The attack was executed by exploiting vulnerabilities in the exchange's infrastructure, leading to the unauthorised transfer of funds to various external wallets.

The attack involved the compromise of private keys associated with Kucoin's hot wallets. This allowed the attackers to initiate a series of transactions, siphoning funds from the exchange. The exact mechanism of the private key compromise remains under investigation, but it is suspected that a combination of phishing and social engineering tactics may have been employed.

Stolen funds were rapidly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple blockchain networks and laundering techniques. The attackers utilised decentralised exchanges (DEXs), mixers, and cross-chain bridges to obfuscate the trail. Notably, funds were routed through the Ethereum and Binance Smart Chain networks, leveraging services like Tornado Cash for mixing.

The attack is attributed to the Lazarus Group, a North Korean state-sponsored hacking group known for targeting cryptocurrency exchanges. This attribution is based on the group's historical tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs), which align with the observed attack patterns.
