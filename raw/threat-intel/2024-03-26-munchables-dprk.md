On 26 March 2024, a significant crypto hack incident, referred to as "Munchables," was executed, resulting in the theft of approximately $62,500,000.00. The attack targeted a blockchain protocol, exploiting vulnerabilities to siphon funds into various wallets. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting both the protocol's operations and its user base.

The attack was executed through a series of transactions that exploited a vulnerability in the protocol's smart contract infrastructure. The specific exploit mechanism remains unidentified, but the pattern suggests potential use of flash loans or reentrancy attacks, common in similar incidents.

Stolen funds were initially moved through a series of rapid transactions across multiple wallets, employing layering techniques such as mixing and bridging. The funds were eventually routed through various exchanges and mixers, including known laundering infrastructure, to obscure their origin.

The attack is suspected to be linked to the hacker group APT38, known for sophisticated cyber operations and financial crimes. The use of advanced laundering techniques and infrastructure overlaps with previous incidents attributed to this group supports this hypothesis.
