On 16 April 2026, Rhea Finance, a decentralised finance (DeFi) protocol, experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $18.4 million USD in various cryptocurrencies. The attack targeted the protocol's smart contracts, exploiting a vulnerability that allowed the attacker to siphon funds from user deposits. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting both the protocol's liquidity and its user base.

The attack was executed through a sophisticated exploitation of a smart contract vulnerability, potentially involving reentrancy or access control failures. The attacker utilised a series of rapid transactions to manipulate the protocol's state, allowing them to withdraw funds without proper authorisation. The exact exploit mechanism remains under investigation, but initial analysis suggests the use of automated scripts to execute the attack efficiently.

Stolen funds were quickly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple wallets and blockchain networks. The attacker employed a combination of bridge hopping and mixer services to obfuscate the fund trail. Key infrastructure used included the Poly Network and various decentralised exchanges (DEXs) for asset swapping. The funds were eventually routed to centralised exchanges (CEXs) for potential cash-out.

The attack is suspected to be the work of the AndAriel group, known for targeting DeFi protocols with similar methodologies. The group's tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) align with those observed in this incident, including the use of sophisticated laundering techniques and infrastructure overlaps with past operations.
