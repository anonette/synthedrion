# Dexible — DPRK-attributed incident

> Amount (USD): $2,000,000
> Asset: Ethereum
> Vector: unknown
> References: Dexible – Blockchain Forensics Intelligence Report (https://sandbox.hacksleuths.com/LLM_REPORTS/Dexible/Dexible_report.html)

On 17 February 2023, the Dexible protocol experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $2,000,000.00. The attack targeted the protocol's smart contracts, exploiting vulnerabilities that allowed unauthorised access to user funds. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting numerous users and stakeholders within the ecosystem.

The attack was executed through a series of transactions that exploited a vulnerability in the Dexible protocol's smart contract. The specific weakness involved was likely related to improper access control or a reentrancy flaw, allowing the attacker to manipulate contract functions to siphon funds.

Stolen funds were rapidly moved from the exploit wallet through a series of intermediary wallets, employing techniques such as bridge hopping and mixer usage to obfuscate the trail. The funds were eventually directed towards centralised exchanges for cash-out. Key infrastructure used included known mixers and cross-chain bridges.

The attack is suspected to be linked to the Lazarus Group, a well-known cybercriminal organisation with a history of targeting cryptocurrency platforms. This attribution is supported by the use of similar tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) observed in previous incidents attributed to this group.
