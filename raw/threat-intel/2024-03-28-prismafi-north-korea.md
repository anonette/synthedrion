On 28 March 2024, PrismaFi, a decentralised finance (DeFi) protocol, experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $11.6 million USD. The attack targeted the protocol's smart contracts, exploiting a vulnerability that allowed the attacker to drain funds from user accounts. The immediate financial impact was severe, affecting both the protocol's liquidity and user trust.

The attack was executed through a sophisticated exploit of the smart contract's logic, potentially involving a reentrancy attack or a flash loan manipulation. The exact vulnerability exploited remains under investigation, but initial analysis suggests a failure in access control mechanisms or improper handling of contract state changes.

Stolen funds were rapidly moved through a series of transactions across multiple blockchain networks. The attacker utilised various laundering techniques, including bridge hopping and mixer services, to obfuscate the fund flow. Key infrastructure used included known bridges and decentralised exchanges (DEXs), with funds eventually reaching centralised exchanges (CEXs) for potential cash-out.

The attack is suspected to be the work of a sophisticated threat actor, potentially linked to previous incidents involving similar tactics and infrastructure. The use of advanced laundering techniques and the speed of execution suggest a high level of expertise and pre-planning.
