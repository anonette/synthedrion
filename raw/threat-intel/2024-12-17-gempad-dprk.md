On 17 December 2024, the GemPad platform experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $1,900,000.00. The attack targeted the platform's smart contract infrastructure, exploiting vulnerabilities to siphon funds into attacker-controlled wallets. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting both the platform's liquidity and its user base.

The attack was executed through a series of coordinated transactions exploiting a vulnerability in the smart contract's access control mechanisms. The attacker utilised a combination of flash loans and reentrancy attacks to manipulate token balances and extract funds without triggering security alerts. This sophisticated method indicates a high level of technical expertise and premeditated planning.

Stolen funds were rapidly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple blockchain networks and laundering techniques. The attacker employed bridge hopping and mixer services to obfuscate the fund trail. Key infrastructure used included the Poly Network and various decentralised exchanges (DEXs) for token swaps, effectively layering the stolen assets to hinder traceability.

The attack is suspected to be orchestrated by the threat group APT38, known for its advanced persistent threat capabilities and previous involvement in similar incidents. The use of specific laundering techniques and infrastructure overlaps with known APT38 operations, supporting this attribution with medium confidence.
