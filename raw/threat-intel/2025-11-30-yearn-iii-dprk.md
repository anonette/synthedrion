On 30 November 2025, the Yearn Finance protocol experienced a significant security breach, resulting in the theft of approximately $9,000,000.00. The attack was executed by the hacker group identified as APT38, known for their sophisticated cyber operations. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting the protocol's liquidity and user trust.

The attack exploited a vulnerability within the Yearn Finance smart contracts, potentially involving a reentrancy attack or a flash loan exploit. The specific technical details of the vulnerability remain under investigation, but the rapid sequence of transactions suggests a well-coordinated attack leveraging automated scripts.

Stolen funds were initially moved from the exploit wallet to intermediary wallets, employing a series of rapid transactions to obfuscate the trail. The funds were then laundered through various blockchain bridges and mixers, including the use of the Yearn - III bridge. The final destinations included several centralised exchanges, where the funds were likely converted to fiat currency.

APT38 is suspected to be behind this attack, with a high confidence level due to the use of known TTPs and infrastructure overlaps with previous incidents attributed to this group. Their modus operandi includes targeting financial institutions and using advanced laundering techniques to obscure fund origins.
