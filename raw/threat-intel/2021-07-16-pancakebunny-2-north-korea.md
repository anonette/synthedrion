On 19 May 2021, the PancakeBunny protocol suffered a significant exploit resulting in the theft of approximately $45 million in digital assets. The attack targeted the protocol's smart contracts on the Binance Smart Chain, exploiting vulnerabilities that allowed the attacker to manipulate token prices and execute a series of transactions that drained funds from the protocol.

The attack was executed through a flash loan exploit, a common vulnerability in DeFi protocols where an attacker borrows a large amount of funds without collateral, manipulates the market, and repays the loan within a single transaction. This method exploited the PancakeBunny smart contract's price calculation mechanism, allowing the attacker to artificially inflate the price of assets and withdraw excessive amounts.

Post-exploit, the stolen funds were rapidly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple wallets and exchanges. The attacker utilised various DeFi protocols and bridges to obfuscate the fund trail, including swapping assets across different chains and using mixers to launder the proceeds.

The identity of the attacker remains unknown, but the sophistication of the attack suggests involvement of a well-resourced and experienced threat actor. The use of advanced laundering techniques and infrastructure overlaps with previous incidents indicate potential links to known cybercriminal groups.
