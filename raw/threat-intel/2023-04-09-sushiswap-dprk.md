On 9 April 2023, SushiSwap, a decentralised finance (DeFi) protocol operating on the Ethereum blockchain, experienced a significant security breach. The incident involved the unauthorised transfer of approximately $3,300,000.00 in cryptocurrency assets from the protocol's liquidity pools. The exploit was executed by a threat actor group identified as APT38, known for their sophisticated cyber operations.

The attack exploited a vulnerability within SushiSwap's smart contract infrastructure, potentially involving a reentrancy attack or a flash loan exploit. The specific technical details of the vulnerability remain under investigation, but the rapid execution of transactions suggests a premeditated and well-coordinated attack.

Stolen funds were initially moved through a series of rapid transactions across multiple wallets, employing techniques such as bridge hopping and mixer usage to obfuscate the trail. The funds were eventually routed through various decentralised exchanges (DEXs) and centralised exchanges (CEXs) for laundering and cash-out purposes.

The attack is attributed to APT38, a group with a history of targeting financial institutions and cryptocurrency platforms. The attribution is supported by the use of similar tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) observed in previous incidents linked to this group.
