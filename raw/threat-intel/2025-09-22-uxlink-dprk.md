# UXLink — DPRK-attributed incident

The UXLink incident involved a sophisticated cyber attack targeting a blockchain-based protocol, resulting in the theft of significant digital assets. The attack occurred on 22 September 2025, affecting the UXLink platform, a decentralised finance (DeFi) protocol with a substantial Total Value Locked (TVL) and a large user base. The immediate financial impact was severe, with millions in digital assets siphoned off through a series of complex transactions.

The attack exploited a vulnerability within the UXLink protocol's smart contract infrastructure. The attackers utilised a combination of flash loans and reentrancy attacks to manipulate the protocol's logic, allowing them to drain funds without triggering immediate security alerts. The exploit involved multiple smart contract functions, including those related to asset swaps and liquidity provision.

Stolen funds were rapidly moved from the exploit wallet through a series of intermediary wallets, employing techniques such as bridge hopping and mixer usage to obfuscate the trail. The funds traversed multiple blockchain networks and utilised decentralised exchanges (DEXs) for asset swapping. Ultimately, portions of the funds were cashed out through centralised exchanges (CEXs), with some assets still in transit.

The attack is suspected to be orchestrated by the Lazarus Group, a well-known cybercrime syndicate with a history of targeting blockchain platforms. This attribution is supported by the use of similar tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) observed in previous incidents attributed to this group. The confidence level in this attribution is medium, pending further investigation.
