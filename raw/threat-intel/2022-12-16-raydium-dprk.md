On 16 December 2022, the Raydium protocol, a decentralised finance (DeFi) platform operating on the Solana blockchain, experienced a significant security breach. The incident involved the unauthorised transfer of approximately $4.4 million in digital assets. The exploit was executed by the Lazarus Group, a threat actor known for its sophisticated cyber operations.

The attack exploited a vulnerability in the Raydium protocol's smart contract infrastructure. The specific weakness involved improper access control mechanisms, allowing the attacker to manipulate transaction flows and extract funds without detection. The exploit was executed using a series of rapid transactions, leveraging the protocol's liquidity pools.

Stolen funds were initially moved through a series of intermediary wallets, employing techniques such as bridge hopping and mixer usage to obfuscate the trail. The funds were subsequently transferred across multiple blockchain networks, including Ethereum and Binance Smart Chain, before being deposited into centralised exchanges for cash-out.

The Lazarus Group is suspected of orchestrating the attack, with a high confidence level based on the use of known tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) associated with the group. These include the use of sophisticated laundering techniques and infrastructure overlaps with previous incidents attributed to the group.
