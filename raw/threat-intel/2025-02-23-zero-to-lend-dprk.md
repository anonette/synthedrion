On 23 February 2025, a significant crypto hack incident, referred to as "Zero To Lend," was executed against a decentralised finance (DeFi) protocol. The attack resulted in the theft of approximately $371,000 USD equivalent in digital assets. The exploit was identified as a sophisticated breach involving multiple transactions and addresses, with the Lazarus Group suspected as the threat actor.

The attack exploited a vulnerability within the protocol's smart contract infrastructure, potentially involving reentrancy or access control failures. The exact mechanism remains under investigation, but initial analysis suggests the use of automated scripts to execute rapid transactions across multiple wallets.

Stolen funds were quickly moved through a series of intermediary wallets, employing techniques such as bridge hopping and mixer usage to obfuscate the trail. The funds traversed multiple blockchain networks and utilised decentralised exchanges (DEXs) and centralised exchanges (CEXs) for laundering.

The Lazarus Group, a North Korean state-sponsored cybercrime organisation, is suspected due to the use of known tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) associated with their previous operations. The group's involvement is supported by transaction patterns and infrastructure overlaps.
