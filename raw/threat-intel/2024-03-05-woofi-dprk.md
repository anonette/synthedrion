On 5 March 2024, the Woofi protocol experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $85 million USD. The attack targeted the protocol's smart contract infrastructure, exploiting vulnerabilities that allowed the unauthorised transfer of funds. The immediate financial impact was severe, affecting both the protocol's liquidity and its user base.

The attack was executed through a sophisticated exploitation of smart contract vulnerabilities, potentially involving reentrancy or access control failures. The exact mechanism remains under investigation, but initial analysis suggests the use of automated scripts to manipulate contract functions and drain funds rapidly.

Stolen funds were quickly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple blockchain networks and laundering techniques. The attacker utilised bridges and mixers to obfuscate the fund trail, with significant portions routed through high-volume intermediaries and eventually reaching centralised exchanges for cash-out.

The attack is suspected to be orchestrated by the hacker group "AndAriel," known for similar exploits in the past. The group's tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) align with those observed in this incident, including the use of complex laundering strategies and infrastructure overlaps with previous attacks.
