On 9 March 2021, a significant crypto hack incident targeted the DODO protocol, resulting in the theft of approximately $2,000,000.00 USD. The attack was executed by exploiting a vulnerability within the protocol's smart contracts, leading to the unauthorised transfer of funds. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting both the protocol's operations and its user base.

The attack exploited a vulnerability in the DODO protocol's smart contracts, potentially involving a reentrancy attack or a flash loan exploit. These types of attacks typically involve manipulating the contract's logic to withdraw more funds than deposited or to execute multiple transactions before the contract's state is updated.

Stolen funds were initially moved from the exploit wallet to several intermediary wallets, employing a series of rapid transactions to obfuscate the trail. The funds were then layered through various mixers and bridges, including the use of ARSI EXCHANGE, identified in the Paradise Papers, suggesting potential money laundering routes.

The Lazarus Group, a well-known cybercrime syndicate, is suspected of orchestrating the attack. This attribution is based on the use of similar tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) observed in previous incidents attributed to the group, as well as infrastructure overlaps.
