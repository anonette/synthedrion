On 1 November 2022, Deribit, a prominent cryptocurrency derivatives exchange, experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $28 million USD in digital assets. The attack was executed through a sophisticated exploit targeting the exchange's infrastructure, leading to the unauthorised transfer of funds from Deribit's wallets. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting both the exchange's operations and its user base.

The attack leveraged a vulnerability in Deribit's wallet management system, potentially involving compromised private keys or a flaw in the access control mechanisms. The attacker executed a series of transactions that rapidly drained funds from the exchange's hot wallets. The precise technical details of the exploit remain under investigation, but the use of automated scripts and infrastructure weaknesses are suspected.

Following the exploit, the stolen funds were swiftly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple intermediary wallets. The attacker utilised a combination of blockchain bridges and mixers to obfuscate the fund trail, eventually directing the assets to various exchanges for cash-out. Notable infrastructure used includes the Ethereum and Binance Smart Chain networks, with funds passing through known laundering services.

The Lazarus Group, a cybercrime organisation linked to North Korea, is the primary suspect in this incident. This attribution is supported by high-confidence matches in offshore leaks databases and the group's known tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) that align with the observed attack patterns.
