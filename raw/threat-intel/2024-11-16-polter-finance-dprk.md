On 16 November 2024, Polter Finance, a decentralised finance (DeFi) protocol, experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $17.4 million USD. The attack was executed by exploiting a vulnerability within the protocol's smart contract infrastructure, leading to unauthorised fund transfers. The immediate financial impact was severe, affecting both the protocol's liquidity and its user base.

The attack leveraged a smart contract vulnerability, potentially involving a reentrancy attack or a flash loan exploit, which allowed the attacker to manipulate the protocol's logic to siphon funds. The specific technical details of the vulnerability remain under investigation, but the rapid execution suggests a premeditated and well-coordinated attack.

Stolen funds were initially moved through a series of rapid transactions across multiple wallets, employing techniques such as bridge hopping and mixer usage to obfuscate the trail. The funds were subsequently transferred through various blockchain networks and potentially laundered through centralised exchanges. Key infrastructure used includes high-volume intermediaries and known laundering services.

The attack is suspected to be orchestrated by the hacker group APT38, known for sophisticated cyber operations and financial crimes. This attribution is based on the use of similar tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) observed in previous incidents linked to the group. The confidence level in this attribution is medium, pending further evidence.
