# Seneca Protocol — DPRK-attributed incident

> Timestamp: 2024-02-28T00:00:00Z

> Amount (USD): $6,400,000
> Asset: Ethereum
> Vector: unknown
> References: Seneca Protocol – Blockchain Forensics Intelligence Report (https://sandbox.hacksleuths.com/LLM_REPORTS/Seneca_Protocol/Seneca_Protocol_report.html)

On 28 February 2024, the Seneca Protocol, a decentralised finance (DeFi) platform, experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $6.4 million USD. The attack targeted the protocol's smart contracts, exploiting vulnerabilities to siphon funds into attacker-controlled wallets. The immediate financial impact was severe, affecting both the protocol's liquidity and its user base.

The attack was executed through a sophisticated exploitation of smart contract vulnerabilities, potentially involving reentrancy attacks or flash loan manipulations. The exact exploit mechanism remains under investigation, but initial analysis suggests a failure in access control mechanisms or improper validation of input data.

Stolen funds were rapidly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple blockchain networks and laundering techniques. The attacker utilised known bridges and mixers to obfuscate the fund trail, including the use of offshore entities and high-volume intermediaries. The funds were eventually distributed across various exchanges and wallets, complicating recovery efforts.

The attack is suspected to be linked to the APT38 group, known for its sophisticated cyber operations and previous involvement in similar incidents. The use of specific laundering techniques and infrastructure overlaps with past APT38 activities supports this attribution. Confidence in this assessment is medium, pending further investigation.
