On 16 October 2024, Radiant Capital, a decentralised finance (DeFi) protocol, experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $53 million USD. The attack targeted the protocol's smart contracts, exploiting vulnerabilities that allowed the attacker to siphon funds from the platform. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting both the protocol's liquidity and its user base.

The attack was executed by exploiting a vulnerability within the smart contract infrastructure of Radiant Capital. Although the specific exploit mechanism is not detailed in the available data, typical vulnerabilities in DeFi protocols include reentrancy attacks, flash loan exploits, and price oracle manipulation. The absence of detailed technical data in the source files limits the precise identification of the exploited weakness.

Post-exploit, the stolen funds were rapidly moved through a series of transactions involving multiple intermediary wallets. The funds were likely laundered through various blockchain bridges and mixers to obscure their origin before reaching final cash-out destinations. The use of multiple chains and potential decentralised exchanges (DEXs) suggests a sophisticated laundering strategy designed to evade detection.

The identity of the threat actor remains unknown, with no specific group or individual currently attributed to the attack. The use of advanced laundering techniques and the rapid movement of funds suggest a high level of sophistication, potentially linking the attack to organised cybercriminal groups known for targeting DeFi platforms.
