On 12 December 2025, the Aevo protocol experienced a significant security breach resulting in the theft of approximately $2.7 million USD equivalent in various cryptocurrencies. The attack targeted Aevo's smart contract infrastructure, exploiting a vulnerability that allowed the attacker to siphon funds from the protocol's reserves. The immediate financial impact was substantial, affecting both the protocol's liquidity and its user base.

The attack was executed through a sophisticated exploit of Aevo's smart contract, potentially involving a reentrancy vulnerability or a flash loan attack. The attacker manipulated the contract's logic to withdraw funds repeatedly before the contract's state could be updated. This type of attack indicates a deep understanding of the protocol's code and potential weaknesses.

Stolen funds were initially moved through a series of rapid transactions across multiple wallets, employing techniques such as bridge hopping and mixer usage to obfuscate the trail. The funds were eventually routed through several blockchain networks, including Ethereum and Binance Smart Chain, before reaching centralised exchanges for cash-out.

The Lazarus Group, a well-known cybercrime syndicate, is suspected of orchestrating the attack. This attribution is based on the group's historical use of similar tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs), as well as infrastructure overlaps with previous incidents attributed to them. The confidence level in this attribution is medium.
