# Clayton Tucker campaign: "Stop AI Data Centers" (Texas Agriculture Commissioner race)

> Source: https://www.claytontuckertx.com/stop_ai_centers
> Collected: 2026-07-19
> Topic Tags: infrastructure, energy, water, local-resistance, us-domestic-politics

## Summary

A Texas Agriculture Commissioner campaign platform built around opposing AI data-center buildout — a grassroots/electoral-politics case of the "social-license backlash" already noted in this wiki's energy page, with concrete local specifics.

## Specific project named

**Project Matador** (Amarillo, TX) — campaign claims that despite being marketed as "nuclear powered," the project is "now building the world's second largest natural gas plant" to power it.

## Core arguments made

- **Resource consumption**: claims data centers caused electricity prices to rise 70% in some areas; water wells running dry in arid regions; even closed-loop cooling systems consume large water volumes.
- **Economic**: claims of inflated costs for electronics/RAM/components; minimal net job creation (automation destroys more jobs than construction creates); benefits concentrated in Wall Street/Big Tech over local communities; threatens prime farmland.
- **Proposed policy response**: "Ag Impact Studies" requiring transparency and slowing development; "Ag Development Districts" to help communities organize local resistance.

## Why it matters for the wiki

A live example of AI infrastructure becoming a mainstream electoral issue at the state level in the US — not just a technical grid/permitting dispute, but a campaign platform. Useful alongside the Imperial Valley case (Avi Spitz essay, same folder) as a second concrete instance of community/political resistance to data-center siting.
