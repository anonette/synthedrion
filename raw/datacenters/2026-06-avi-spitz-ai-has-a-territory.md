# AI Has a Territory: Data Centers, Sovereignty, and the Geopolitics of AI Infrastructure

> Source: Student essay, Avi Spitz (Bar-Ilan University), submitted June 2026
> Collected: 2026-07-19
> Topic Tags: infrastructure, sovereignty, energy, geopolitics

## Summary

Central claim: AI sovereignty cannot be reduced to control over models, data, or regulation — it depends on operational control over a physical, energy-intensive, often privately-owned compute stack (data centers, chips, electricity, cooling, fiber, cloud contracts, security arrangements). Coins the term **infrastructural suzerainty**: a state may hold formal/legal sovereignty over AI within its borders while practical control sits with foreign chip suppliers, cloud providers, or external corporations — "the state has formal sovereignty, while infrastructure creates dependence."

Draws on Kate Crawford's *Atlas of AI* (AI as an extractive system: minerals, labor, data, energy) and Susan Leigh Star's infrastructure studies (infrastructure is invisible until it breaks, is scarce, or becomes contested).

## Key concrete cases cited

- **Gulf compute hubs**: Stargate UAE (Abu Dhabi) — first 200MW phase beginning operation 2026, broader plan for 1GW (OpenAI 2025). Saudi Arabia: AWS Saudi cloud region planned 2026; HUMAIN (backed by the Public Investment Fund) building data-center capacity in Riyadh and Dammam.
- **Imperial Valley water-rights conflict** (California): after recycled-water negotiations stalled, Imperial Valley Computer Manufacturing sued the Imperial Irrigation District for access to ~260 million gallons/year of Colorado River water; local reporting found the project would require more electricity than all of Imperial County consumed in 2024 (McLean, SFGATE, June 2026).
- **New/contested geographies of compute**: floating data centers at sea, orbital/space-based data centers — proposed as land, electricity, cooling, security, and local-opposition constraints push infrastructure toward new territories (SpaceX orbital AI compute tests targeted for 2027).
- **IEA projection**: global data-center electricity consumption could more than double to ~945 TWh by 2030 (IEA, *Energy and AI*, 2025) — consistent with figures already in this wiki's energy page.

## Framework: the AI infrastructure stack (layers, top to bottom)

AI models/applications → cloud platforms & APIs → data centers & compute clusters → chips/accelerators/software stack → electricity/cooling/water/land/fiber → jurisdiction/ownership/security/crisis access. Sovereignty claims made at the top layer (a "national AI strategy") can be hollow if the bottom layers are foreign-controlled.

## Regulatory implication argued

AI regulation should expand beyond model outputs/risk/privacy to also cover: where compute is sited, who owns it, what energy/water systems support it, security standards, and crisis-access rules. Data centers should be treated as strategic infrastructure (like ports, grids, undersea cables) rather than ordinary private real estate — without being nationalized.
